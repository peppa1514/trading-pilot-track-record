What is this?

A computer program trades 8 cryptocurrencies with pretend money, but at real market prices and with real fees. No AI decides anything: it follows fixed rules written in advance. Everything is published here every day, losses included. This is not investment advice.

How it decides (plain words)

Once a day, after the daily price closes, the program checks each crypto. If one of its four methods sees a signal, it opens a trade with a stop-loss (a price at which it accepts a small loss and gets out).

  • Method A buys when the price closes at its highest level of the last 50 days in a rising market, and stays in as long as the trend lasts. (Andreas Clenow)
  • Method B buys a dip inside a rising trend, with a stop-loss below and a target three times further than the risk. (Thami Kabbaj)
  • Method C buys when the price closes above its highest point of the last 20 days, and lets the stop-loss follow the price up. (Van K. Tharp)
  • Method D waits for a short pullback in a rising trend, buys when the price makes a new 20-day high, and aims for three times the risk. (Van K. Tharp)

Each trade risks 0.75% of the account (about $7.50), and never more than 5% is at risk in total. One trade per crypto at a time.

The same methods can also sell short (bet on a fall), mirror-image. Methods A, B and D only do it in a downtrend: price below its 200-day average (for B and D, the shorter averages must point down too). Method C can also sell short when the price breaks below its 20-day low.

Words used on this page

R (the result unit)

1R is the amount a trade risks when it opens (about $7.50 here). A trade that ends at −1R lost what it planned to risk; +3R means it won three times that amount. It lets you compare trades of different sizes.

Stop-loss

A price fixed in advance at which the program accepts a loss and gets out, so one bad trade cannot do much damage.

Take-profit (target)

A price fixed in advance at which the program takes its gain and gets out.

Drawdown (dip)

How far the account has fallen from its highest point. A −6% dip means it went down 6% from a peak before (maybe) recovering.

Long / short

Long = buy, and gain if the price rises. Short = "sell short": borrow and sell, then buy back later, which gains if the price falls.

Funding

The program trades "perpetual futures", contracts that follow the price of a crypto. Every 8 hours, buyers and sellers of these contracts pay each other a small fee called funding. It is counted in the results.

Backtest vs live

A backtest replays rules on past prices. It is easy to fool yourself with it, because you already know the past. Live results come from prices that did not exist when the rules were fixed: only those really count.

Why simulated?

To build an honest, public, day-by-day record first, without risking anyone's money. Prices, fees and funding are real (Binance Futures); no order is sent to an exchange.

Honesty box

In a test on past prices, these rules made far less than simply holding bitcoin.

Published failures: we tested 6 AI models as a filter on 300 past trade signals, and none did better than simply taking every signal (AI veto study). That is why no AI decides here. Every losing trade stays in the data.
